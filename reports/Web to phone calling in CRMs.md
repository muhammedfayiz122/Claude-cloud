# Web clicks dial phones through push and taps

Across the market, "click Call in the web app and my own phone places the call" works the same way wherever it exists. The web app asks the server to send a **push notification (FCM on Android, APNs on iOS) to the user's logged-in vendor mobile app**. The user **taps the notification**. The app then hands the number to the native dialer or places the SIM call itself. Only a few vendors document this as a first-party feature: **Pipedrive ("Web-to-mobile calling") and HubSpot ("Call from HubSpot app")** among global CRMs, and **LeadSquared, Kylas, TeleCRM, NeoDove and Callyzer** among Android-first Indian SMB CRMs. Everyone else uses a browser softphone, a server-side callback bridge (RingCentral RingOut, HubSpot "Call from phone"), or an OS relay (Phone Link, Continuity) that a web app cannot drive. The weakest part of every documented product is the feedback loop. **No vendor documents a live "ringing on your phone / in call 02:13" panel on the web, a delivery acknowledgement, or a distinct "phone locked/offline" state.** The best public example is LeadSquared's "No Target Device Retrieved. Cannot Call", which only says that no device is registered. Platform rules explain most of this. Android 10+ blocks background activity launches. Android 14 restricts full-screen intents to calling and alarm apps. Google Play limits call-log access. iOS always requires a confirmation tap for `tel:` calls. Neither OS lets a non-dialer app see more than coarse call state, and FCM gives the sender no real-time delivery receipt. For an enterprise no-code ecosystem, the lesson is to build device registry, push, presence and a command lifecycle (request → delivered → accepted → dialing → ended / expired) as **shared, tenant-aware platform services**. These services carry minimal payloads, rely on app-level acknowledgements and short expiry, and power a shell-level call widget. The CRM is then one consumer among several.

## Four architectures compete, and only one uses the vendor app on the user's phone

The products in this space use four delivery models, and the choice decides almost everything else. The first is **push-to-app, then native/carrier dial**. Pipedrive's web flow is: click a number, choose "Start the call from Pipedrive mobile app", click "Start call". The phone then shows a notification "saying the call was started from the web app", and tapping it lets the user continue the call on the phone. Calls bill to the user's normal carrier plan, and the feature requires **Android 11+ or iOS 18+**, notifications enabled, and on Android "calling must be enabled on the device" ([Pipedrive KB](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/web-to-mobile-calling)). HubSpot's calling tool has a Device menu with three options: "Call from browser", "Call from phone" and "Call from HubSpot app". The last one "sends a push notification to your phone so you can start the call in the HubSpot mobile app" ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/calling/use-the-calling-tool)). LeadSquared is the only vendor that documents the plumbing. A web action "sends a call nudge (a mobile notification) to the user's phone through the Nudges Connector", the notification "is silent", and the setup is built by an admin from connectors, a nudge template of type "Call Routing", and a grid action that POSTs to an API with ProspectID and UserId ([LeadSquared help](https://help.leadsquared.com/?p=8755)). Kylas ("A call to the Lead's or Contact's number will be placed from your mobile phone", all plans) and TeleCRM ("Click any lead's number in the browser — dials on mobile instantly", using "the phone's native dialler") follow the same pattern on Android ([Kylas KB](https://support.kylas.io/portal/en/kb/articles/how-to-make-outbound-call-from-kylas-web-application-via-your-mobile-phone); [TeleCRM blog](https://telecrm.in/blog/click-to-call-crm/)). NeoDove calls it "GSM calling triggered to mobile" ([NeoDove FAQ](https://docs.neodove.com/neodove-web-application/faqs-web-application)).

The second model is the **server-side callback bridge**. The cloud PBX calls the user's registered phone, then connects the customer. RingCentral RingOut "calls your RingOut number first". The user answers, presses 1 if prompted, and then the other party is connected with the RingCentral number as caller ID ([RingCentral support](https://support.ringcentral.com/app/phone/desktop-web/enabling-using-ringout-ringcentral-app-desktop-web.html)). HubSpot's "Call from phone" "calls your registered phone number" and "uses twice as many calling minutes" because it places two calls ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/calling/use-the-calling-tool)). Some Zoho PhoneBridge providers work the same way ([Telebroad](https://helpdesk.telebroad.com/support/solutions/articles/4000171309-zoho-phonebridge)). The bridge needs no app, works when the app is closed, and gives the server full call state for free. Its costs are two billed legs, a cloud caller ID instead of the user's SIM identity, and an answer-confirmation step. RingCentral community users report that without that step the second leg can connect before the user picks up, so the customer hears voicemail ([RingCentral community](https://community.ringcentral.com/developer-platform-apis-integrations-5/what-is-the-flow-of-creating-ringout-for-users-714)).

The third model, used by most enterprise and telephony products, treats the **mobile app as just another VoIP endpoint**. Salesforce Sales Dialer runs WebRTC in a utility-bar panel ([Trailhead](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/lightning-dialer-basics/get-started-with-lightning-dialer)). One secondary source reports that it is being retired in favour of Salesforce Voice ([Aloware](https://aloware.com/blog/what-salesforce-dialer-is-and-how-to-choose-one)). Dynamics 365's Teams dialer never mentions starting a call on mobile ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/sales/call-using-microsoft-teams)). Close and Freshcaller ring the mobile app together with desktop ([Close](https://help.close.com/docs/close-mobile-app); [Freshworks](https://freshworks.com/freshcaller-cloud-pbx/call-center-performance/freshcaller-mobile-app-library)). monday Smart Call triggers a call on the phone from desktop, but on a business number bought inside monday, not the SIM ([monday support](https://support.monday.com/hc/en-us/articles/35893698534674-Get-started-with-monday-Smart-Call)). The fourth model is **OS-level relay**. Phone Link (Bluetooth HFP), Apple Continuity (same Apple Account, same Wi-Fi, about 10 m) and Samsung "Call & text on other devices" let a paired phone place a real cellular call and relay the audio ([Microsoft](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/help/4531605/setting-up-and-using-calls); [Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/receive-calls-text-messages-mac-mchl1b152152/mac); [Samsung US](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS00083152/)). A web app cannot invoke these. Chrome's removed click-to-call is the closest historical precedent for the push-to-app model: it sent an FCM message with a `ClickToCallMessage` payload to the user's own signed-in Android Chrome, addressed by FCM token plus encryption keys ([Chromium sharing README](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/chrome/browser/sharing/README.md); [sharing_message.proto](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/689912289c/chrome/browser/sharing/proto/sharing_message.proto)). Google disabled it by default in 2023 and removed the flag in Chrome 116 ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2023/08/30/google-chrome-click-to-call-feature-removed/)).

## Pairing is just login, and nobody documents multi-device routing

No vendor uses an explicit pairing ceremony for push-to-app calling. **Pairing is implicit: being logged into the mobile app as the same user with notifications enabled.** Kylas asks only that the user log into the same account on web and mobile ([Kylas KB](https://support.kylas.io/portal/en/kb/articles/how-to-make-outbound-call-from-kylas-web-application-via-your-mobile-phone)). LeadSquared's fix for "No Target Device Retrieved" is to log out and back in, which re-registers the device so it can receive the silent push ([LeadSquared help](https://help.leadsquared.com/?p=8755)). The singular "Target Device" and the routing by UserId suggest one target device per user with last-login-wins, but that is an inference. Bridges "pair" by phone-number registration instead: HubSpot requires users to register a number ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/calling-user-guide/how-to-register-your-phone-number-for-calling)), and RingCentral stores one or more RingOut numbers ([RingCentral](https://support.ringcentral.com/app/phone/desktop-web/enabling-using-ringout-ringcentral-app-desktop-web.html)). OS relays pair through a shared account plus local radio pairing, such as Phone Link's Bluetooth PIN confirmation or Apple's per-device "Calls on Other Devices" allow-list ([Microsoft](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/help/4531605/setting-up-and-using-calls); [Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/receive-calls-text-messages-mac-mchl1b152152/mac)).

Multi-device handling is the biggest documentation gap. **No CRM says which device receives the push when a user has two phones (or a phone and a tablet) logged in.** The likely behaviour is fan-out to all registered devices, first tap wins, but this is unconfirmed. The telephony world offers two reference models. For inbound calls it rings every endpoint at once: Aircall's app and forwarding number "ring simultaneously" with no sequential option ([Aircall](https://support.aircall.io/en-gb/articles/10375354624413)). For outbound calls the user picks: Dialpad's "select your preferred Dialpad device" ([Dialpad](https://help.dialpad.com/hc/en-us/articles/211463286-Enable-Click-to-Call-with-Chrome)), CallHippo's desktop-vs-Chrome choice ([CallHippo](https://callhippo.ladesk.com/871505-Click-to-Call)), and Chrome's per-click "Call from your devices" menu listing the user's handsets ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2019/02/22/google-chrome-click-call-android/)). For an outbound SIM call, fan-out is the wrong default. Two phones lighting up for one call is confusing, and a tablet without a SIM cannot dial. The registry therefore needs per-device capability flags (has telephony, can place call) and a remembered preferred device, with a chooser when it is ambiguous.

## The phone always demands a human tap, by OS rule and store policy

No vendor ships fully unattended dialing. **Pipedrive needs a notification tap plus a "continue" tap, and LeadSquared's notification opens the dialer pre-filled and "You must tap the call button"** ([Pipedrive KB](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/web-to-mobile-calling); [LeadSquared help](https://help.leadsquared.com/?p=8755)). Platform constraints drive this, not just caution.

### Android 10+ and 14 force the notification-tap pattern

Since **Android 10 (API 29), apps cannot start activities from the background**. An FCM handler cannot pop a dialer UI. The exemptions that matter are a system-sent PendingIntent such as a notification tap, a user-granted `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW`, and core OS components such as Telephony showing the in-call screen. Blocked launches fail silently, with only a logcat line ([Android: background starts](https://developer.android.com/guide/components/activities/background-starts)). This explains Kylas's requirement to enable "Display over other apps" (plus a Xiaomi-specific pop-up permission) so its call pop-up appears "even when the app is killed" ([Kylas KB](https://support.kylas.io/portal/en/kb/articles/how-to-make-outbound-call-from-kylas-web-application-via-your-mobile-phone)). Android 14 and 15 tightened PendingIntent rules further: senders and creators must explicitly opt into background-activity-start privileges ([Android: background starts](https://developer.android.com/guide/components/activities/background-starts)). **Android 14 also limits `USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT` to apps whose core function is calling or alarms.** Play revokes the default grant for other apps ([Android 14 behavior changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/behavior-changes-14)). Only calling and alarm apps have it enabled by default from January 22, 2025, and others must "prompt users to grant permission … and gracefully degrade" ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821)). Play's wording is "receiving phone or video calls", so an outbound CRM click-to-call companion should not assume it qualifies. Without the permission, the notification "will show up as an expanded heads up notification on lockscreen" ([NotificationManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/NotificationManager)).

Placing the call itself is easier. `ACTION_DIAL` needs no permission but only pre-fills the dialer. With the runtime **`CALL_PHONE`** permission, `ACTION_CALL` or **`TelecomManager.placeCall()`** dials directly through the system Telecom stack. Emergency numbers are excluded, and a self-managed ConnectionService is VoIP-only ([Intent reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/content/Intent); [TelecomManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/telecom/TelecomManager)). `placeCall` needs no activity launch, so a notification action can trigger it from a broadcast or service. That gives one tap on the phone with no dialer confirmation. Google's docs do not say whether `placeCall` from a pure background component (no tap) is blocked on Android 15/16, so that needs device testing. Even if it works, unattended remote dialing creates toll-fraud and abuse risk. Keeping a human tap is the right design. The `phoneCall` foreground-service type requires `MANAGE_OWN_CALLS` or the dialer role, so a CRM helper that tracks call state should use `shortService` (~3 minutes) or a Play-reviewed `specialUse` type ([FGS types](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types)). Becoming the default dialer (`ROLE_DIALER`) gives full call state and call-log rights, but it replaces the user's phone app. Kylas Dialer offers this as an optional first-launch prompt the user can decline ([Kylas call-log KB](https://support.kylas.io/portal/en/kb/articles/how-to-sync-the-mobile-phone-call-logs-with-kylas)).

### iOS allows exactly one path: alert push, tap, confirm

iOS is stricter and simpler. **Opening a `tel:` URL "displays an alert and requires user confirmation before dialing" (iOS 10.3+)**, only from a foreground app ([Apple Phone Links](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/featuredarticles/iPhoneURLScheme_Reference/PhoneLinks/PhoneLinks.html)). PushKit VoIP pushes are off-limits for this use. Since iOS 13 every VoIP push must be reported to CallKit as an incoming call, or "the system will terminate your app", and repeated failures stop VoIP push delivery altogether ([PushKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/pushkit/pkpushregistrydelegate/pushregistry(_:didreceiveincomingpushwith:for:completion:))). CallKit features are also barred from the China App Store ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2018/05/19/apple-cracking-down-on-callkit-apps-in-china-app-store-due-to-government-regulation/)). The workable iOS flow is a regular APNs alert with the **Time Sensitive** interruption level, which "lights up the screen" and breaks through Focus and Notification Summary unless the user disables it ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/unnotificationinterruptionlevel/timesensitive)). The user taps it, the app comes to the foreground and opens `tel:`, and the user confirms. That is **at least two taps on the phone, plus Face ID or a passcode if the phone is locked**. Pipedrive's iOS 18+ floor hints that it relies on recent iOS behaviour, but the specifics are undocumented.

### Google Play gates sensitive permissions

Play's SMS & Call Log policy allows `READ_CALL_LOG` only for default handlers and listed exceptions. One exception is **"Enterprise archive, CRM, and/or enterprise device management"**, where CRM use is limited to asterisked permissions. The mobile-CRM research reads these as `READ_CALL_LOG` and `PROCESS_OUTGOING_CALLS`, not `WRITE_CALL_LOG`. A second reading of the table could not confirm the asterisks, so check the live table before filing. Apps must submit a Permissions Declaration Form, exceptions apply only where "there's currently no alternative method", and **"call recorder" is explicitly an invalid use** ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10208820)). Vendors handle this structurally. LeadSquared puts tracking and recording in a separate "Call and SMS tracker" app ([LeadSquared](https://help.leadsquared.com/?p=9296)), Kylas ships a separate Dialer app ([Kylas](https://support.kylas.io/portal/en/kb/articles/how-to-sync-the-mobile-phone-call-logs-with-kylas)), and Salestrail distributes a recording APK outside Play ([Salestrail](https://www.salestrail.io/knowledge-base/calls-not-logged-in-the-salestrail-app-causes-and-solutions)). These splits keep the main CRM app's Play review scope small. Apps also attach OEM-recorder files instead of recording in-app, and NeoDove says outright that it cannot record "because of Google's guidelines" ([NeoDove](https://docs.neodove.com/neodove-web-application/faqs-web-application)).

## Call status stops at "off-hook" unless the app gets privileged

Showing live call status in the web UI is limited by what the phone app can observe, and that differs sharply by OS. **A non-dialer Android app with `READ_PHONE_STATE` sees only IDLE, RINGING and OFFHOOK.** OFFHOOK means "at least one call exists that is dialing, active, or on hold" ([TelephonyManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/telephony/TelephonyManager); [CallStateListener](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/telephony/TelephonyCallback.CallStateListener)). The app can report "dispatched → dialing/connected → ended" and an elapsed time that includes ringing, but it **cannot tell answered from unanswered or busy**. Precise call state needs `READ_PRECISE_PHONE_STATE`, which is privileged or carrier-only. True duration and answered/missed come from reading the call log after the call, which needs the Play CRM exception. That is what Kylas Dialer and Salestrail do ([Kylas](https://support.kylas.io/portal/en/kb/articles/how-to-sync-the-mobile-phone-call-logs-with-kylas); [Salestrail](https://www.salestrail.io/knowledge-base/calls-not-logged-in-the-salestrail-app-causes-and-solutions)). TeleCRM claims connected/unanswered/missed status but concedes that "Recording and logging depend on the device and Android version" ([TeleCRM](https://telecrm.in/blog/click-to-call-crm/)).

iOS reverses the trade-off. There is no call-log API, but **`CXCallObserver` lets any app observe system calls**, and `CXCall.hasConnected` means "both caller and callee can start communicating". That is a better "answered" signal than Android's non-privileged OFFHOOK ([CXCallObserver](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/callkit/cxcallobserver); [hasConnected](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/callkit/cxcall/hasconnected)). It exposes no phone number, so the app must correlate by timing ("the first outgoing call after we opened `tel:`"). Reporting is fragile while the Phone app is frontmost. LeadSquared's iOS docs warn that the app must stay in the foreground for the whole call, or "a one-minute call can show as six minutes" ([LeadSquared](https://help.leadsquared.com/?p=9296)). Whether `CXCallObserver` callbacks reach a suspended app during a cellular call is not confirmed by Apple documentation.

Logging practice follows these limits. **Pipedrive shows the call-log prompt in the web app after the call ends**, which implies the phone reports call end to the server ([Pipedrive KB](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/web-to-mobile-calling)). HubSpot's mobile Log Call dialog pre-fills duration, and its Android-only option to log calls made outside the app reflects the platform split ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/calling/make-calls-from-the-hubspot-mobile-app)). Dynamics creates a phone-call activity and skips unanswered calls by default ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/sales/call-using-microsoft-teams)). Close logs `tel:` calls without duration ([Close](https://help.close.com/docs/initiating-calls-from-the-web-app.md)). Bridges and VoIP avoid the problem entirely because the server owns both legs. That is the real reason enterprise CRMs prefer them: **server-observed state is accurate, and phone-observed state is a best-effort reconstruction**.

## Silence is the default failure mode for locked, offline and killed phones

The question users care about most, "why didn't my phone ring?", is the least documented. **Pipedrive documents only the case where the app is not installed (an error prompting download), and LeadSquared documents only "no device registered".** Neither covers locked, offline, Doze-delayed, force-stopped, or notification-ignored phones ([Pipedrive KB](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/web-to-mobile-calling); [LeadSquared](https://help.leadsquared.com/?p=8755)). OS relays are no better. Phone Link troubleshooting is "remove the pairing and pair again" ([Microsoft](https://support.microsoft.com/en-US/Windows/Apps/PhoneLink/troubleshooting-calls-in-the-Phone-Link)), and no OS feature documents a "phone locked" message. Instead, vendors put the burden on users through OEM settings: LeadSquared requires Autostart, and Kylas requires overlay permissions.

The root cause is that **FCM gives the sender no real-time delivery receipt**. Per-message `MESSAGE_DELIVERED` events exist only in an opt-in BigQuery export that requires Google Analytics and takes up to 48 hours to propagate. The Aggregated Delivery Data API is Android-only, aggregated, and delayed up to five days ([FCM: understand delivery](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/understand-delivery)). Messages to force-stopped apps are dropped, and "you likely can't do much about this" ([Firebase blog](https://firebase.blog/posts/2024/07/understand-fcm-delivery-rates/)). Normal-priority messages wait out Doze. High-priority messages wake the device, but FCM **silently downgrades apps whose high-priority messages don't produce visible notifications** (evaluated over 7 days), and a downgraded message cannot start a foreground service, which throws `ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException` ([FCM priority](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/android/message-priority); [FGS restrictions](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start)). LeadSquared's "silent" nudge is risky under this rule. FCM's 2026 v2 API makes the link explicit: `RemoteNotification` exists "to push a notification to the user", backed by a 20-second execution window ([App Standby Buckets](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/appstandby)). OEM battery killers add a long tail. dontkillmyapp rates Huawei, Xiaomi, OnePlus and Samsung 5/5 for severity ([dontkillmyapp](https://dontkillmyapp.com/)). Salestrail reports Android pausing apps after about 6–8 hours, and calls on dual-SIM phones go untracked if no SIM is selected ([Salestrail](https://www.salestrail.io/knowledge-base/calls-not-logged-in-the-salestrail-app-causes-and-solutions)).

So the web can only learn reachability from **application-level acknowledgements with timeouts**. The app POSTs "received" as soon as the push lands, then "notification shown", "user tapped", "dialing" and "ended". Each missing ack within a window maps to a specific message in the web UI. The phone can also say why it is stuck. `KeyguardManager.isDeviceLocked()` distinguishes a secure lock from a swipe lock, and `canUseFullScreenIntent()` and the FCM priority check reveal degraded paths ([KeyguardManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/KeyguardManager)). Before first unlock after a reboot, a non-Direct-Boot-aware app cannot run, so no ack arrives. This is untested in the research and needs device verification. The ack-timeout pattern is how MDM systems already work. Android Management API commands carry an expiry, ten minutes by default, plus error codes, and are tracked as long-running operations ([AMAPI issueCommand](https://developers.google.com/android/management/reference/rest/v1/enterprises.devices/issueCommand)). Apple MDM queues commands by UUID behind a content-free wake push and expects explicit `Acknowledged`/`Error`/`NotNow` replies ([Apple MDM](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/devicemanagement/sending-mdm-commands-to-a-device)); the research could not re-verify this Apple description in this pass. Salestrail's admin dashboard, with per-user "App installed" and "Permissions" health, is the only shipping example of pre-flight device health in this space ([Salestrail](https://www.salestrail.io/knowledge-base/calls-not-logged-in-the-salestrail-app-causes-and-solutions)).

## Web UI: a modal and post-call prompt today, a shell-level widget tomorrow

Documented push-to-phone flows use a **lightweight modal plus a post-call logging prompt, not a live in-call panel**. Pipedrive opens a call-options window with a Phone tab and the mobile option ([Pipedrive KB](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/web-to-mobile-calling)), and HubSpot puts a Device selector in its call pop-up ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/calling/use-the-calling-tool)). Persistent dialer surfaces exist for browser-audio calling: Salesforce's utility-bar softphone, which Open CTI can show or minimize via `setSoftphonePanelVisibility` and resize ([Salesforce Open CTI](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/service/api-cti/guide/sforce-api-cti-setsoftphonepanelvisibility-lex.md)), Dynamics' docked Calls pane, Freshcaller's bottom-right widget ([Freshcaller](https://support.freshcaller.com/support/solutions/articles/50000002981)), and Aircall's detachable always-on-top toolbar on desktop and persistent top call bar on iOS ([Aircall](https://support.aircall.io/en-gb/articles/21534383206685); [Aircall iOS](https://support.aircall.io/en-gb/articles/34768865195933)). The common principle is that the widget lives in the **app shell, outside the product router**, so it survives navigation.

A web-to-phone widget should combine the two: a floating, minimizable card that tracks the request through "Sending to Pixel 8 → Delivered → Waiting for you to tap → Dialing → In call 02:13 → Ended → Log this call". If a draggable floating card is used, **WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.7 (AA) requires a non-drag alternative**, such as dock-left/right buttons or arrow-key movement on a focused handle ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)). Status changes belong in a polite live region, and a non-modal widget must not trap focus. That last point is general practice; the research did not find an ARIA pattern written for this widget. Status should reach the browser as small hints ("call_request X changed, version N") over SSE or WebSocket, followed by a REST refetch, with a sequence number so a late hint never overwrites newer state. Microsoft Graph presence uses the same pattern with `sequenceNumber` and change subscriptions ([Graph presence](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/resources/presence)). One connection per browser can be shared across tabs with BroadcastChannel, which spans "windows, tabs, frames, or iframes and workers on the same origin" ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Broadcast_Channel_API)), and only a leader tab should play sounds.

## Privacy rests on what the push carries and what gets logged

No vendor documents what its call notification shows on the lock screen. Close's inbound caller ID shows lead and contact names and marks the call as coming from Close ([Close](https://help.close.com/docs/close-mobile-app)), and push-handoff notifications most likely show the contact name or number too. Two facts favour minimal payloads. **FCM caps payloads at 4096 bytes** ([Firebase](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/customize-messages/set-message-type)), and push content transits Google and Apple infrastructure. For tenants in jurisdictions with data-transfer rules, such as Saudi Arabia's PDPL transfer regulation of September 2024, putting the callee's number and name in the payload is plausibly a cross-border transfer ([Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2024/09/saudi-arabia-s-personal-data-protection-law-become)). This is an inference, not legal advice. The safer design sends `{type, request_id}` only, and the app fetches details over its authenticated, in-region session. On the lock screen the notification says "Call request from CRM", and the contact name appears only after unlock. Logging scope is a second privacy lever. Kylas logs only calls to numbers that match a Lead or Contact ([Kylas](https://support.kylas.io/portal/en/kb/articles/how-to-sync-the-mobile-phone-call-logs-with-kylas)). Salestrail skips calls outside office hours and on non-selected SIMs ([Salestrail](https://www.salestrail.io/knowledge-base/calls-not-logged-in-the-salestrail-app-causes-and-solutions)). LeadSquared limits recording access to the lead owner, managers and users the lead is shared with ([LeadSquared](https://help.leadsquared.com/?p=9296)). Caller identity is a third lever. SIM calls expose the rep's personal number unless masking is configured ([TeleCRM](https://telecrm.in/blog/click-to-call-crm/)), whereas bridges and monday's business numbers hide it ([RingCentral](https://support.ringcentral.com/app/phone/desktop-web/enabling-using-ringout-ringcentral-app-desktop-web.html)).

## Device registry and push belong to the platform, not the CRM

In an ecosystem where CRM shares identity and sessions with other products, "place a call on my phone" is one instance of a general capability: **send a command to a user's device and track it to completion**. Approvals, MFA prompts, task reminders and document signing need the same plumbing. The large platforms converge on the same split. A platform-level registration object binds a push handle to an app and routing metadata. Azure Notification Hubs calls it an "installation", with idempotent upserts, client-generated stable IDs and backend-managed tags. AWS SNS calls it a "platform endpoint", with idempotent create and disable-on-invalid-token ([Azure](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/notification-hubs/notification-hubs-push-notification-registration-management); [AWS SNS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sns/latest/dg/mobile-platform-endpoint.html)). Products then send to users or tags, never to raw tokens. Azure recommends backend-side tag management because "apps can be hacked", so a client must never claim a `tenant:` or `user:` tag itself ([Azure](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/notification-hubs/notification-hubs-push-notification-registration-management)). Salesforce adds two layers worth copying: **per-app daily push quotas, and custom notification types that a connected app must subscribe to** before it can push ([Salesforce](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/atlas.en-us.pushImplGuide.meta/pns_limits.htm), seen via search snippet). Zoho shows the fragmented alternative: push credentials per Creator application, with cross-app aggregation added later in the Workplace dashboard ([Zoho Creator](https://www.zoho.com.cn/creator/help/application/push-notifications.html); [Zoho Workplace](https://www.zoho.com/workplace/collaboration-dashboard.html)).

**Tenant isolation of push credentials** depends on who owns the app binary. For the vendor's own first-party app, a single platform-owned FCM project and APNs key is right. The tenant is then a routing attribute, and quota and abuse monitoring stay central. Per-tenant credentials are needed only for white-label or tenant-built apps, as in the Zoho Creator model, and they must sit in a per-tenant secret store, never in product databases. Isolation then has to hold at the authorization layer. The push only says "request X exists". The app re-authenticates and fetches X, and the server checks that X belongs to the device's current user and tenant. That check defeats a stale push reaching a handle that has been re-bound to another user on a shared device. Registry hygiene follows Firebase guidance ([Firebase: manage tokens](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/manage-tokens)). Store installation rows keyed by an installation ID with timestamps. Delete on `UNREGISTERED`/404, and treat `INVALID_ARGUMENT` with care because it can also mean a bad payload. Prune stale rows daily, at around 30 days, given that FCM treats a month offline as stale and Android expires registrations after 270 days. Also enforce uniqueness on the push handle, so a re-login on a shared device moves the row instead of duplicating it, and revoke bindings when platform SSO ends a session. That last step is good practice that the sources do not spell out.

Presence should be **session-based with decay**, as in Microsoft Graph. Each client holds a presence session, the user's presence is the aggregate, `Available` decays after 5 minutes without a refresh, and expiry is configurable from 5 minutes to 4 hours ([Graph presence](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/cloud-communications-manage-presence-state)). For phones that cannot heartbeat reliably in the background, the honest UI is "last seen 3 min ago", not a green dot. **Per-tenant enablement and admin policy** should stack in layers. Each layer is attested in some product, though the full stack is a synthesis. Platform entitlement comes first, then tenant admin enable, as with Odoo's tenant-level push toggle ([Viindoo](https://viindoo.com/docs/13.0/d/mobile-329)). Next is role permission, then user device opt-in and per-type notification preferences, as in Slack's consolidated modes and Zoho Workplace's per-app toggles ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/04/slack-new-notification-system/)). Admin policy knobs come last: which platforms and app versions are allowed, the maximum number of devices per user, whether managed devices are required, whether the dialing path is direct or via the dialer, and tracking scope and retention. LeadSquared's per-role Track Calls / Record Calls settings with "Override settings on device" show such admin enforcement already shipping ([LeadSquared](https://help.leadsquared.com/?p=9296)).

### Concrete architecture lessons and recommendations

| Area | Recommendation | Grounding |
|---|---|---|
| Service boundaries | Platform owns **Device Registry**, **Notification/Push**, **Presence**, and **Device Command** services. CRM registers a `call.place` command type and a `call.status` event type and never touches tokens | Azure/SNS registration objects; Salesforce notification types |
| Registry schema | `installation_id (client UUID) · tenant · user · platform_session · platform · push_handle (encrypted, unique) · app/OS version · device_label · capabilities{has_telephony, can_place_call, fsi_granted, notif_enabled} · status · last_seen · last_push_error` | [Firebase](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/manage-tokens); [AWS SNS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sns/latest/dg/mobile-platform-endpoint.html) |
| Command lifecycle | `created → pushed → delivered(ack) → shown → accepted(tap) → dialing(OFFHOOK / CXCall outgoing) → connected(iOS hasConnected or call-log duration >0) → ended`, with side exits `declined · failed(reason) · expired`. Idempotent by request ID; one active request per user | [AMAPI](https://developers.google.com/android/management/reference/rest/v1/enterprises.devices/issueCommand); Apple MDM |
| Expiry | Push TTL ~30–60 s (or 0) plus server-side `expires_at`, so a stale "call now" never fires minutes later | [FCM lifespan](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/customize-messages/setting-message-lifespan) |
| Push class | User-visible, time-sensitive alert (FCM high / v2 `RemoteNotification`, APNs Time Sensitive); never silent, never PushKit VoIP | [FCM priority](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/android/message-priority); [PushKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/pushkit/pkpushregistrydelegate/pushregistry(_:didreceiveincomingpushwith:for:completion:)) |
| Payload | `{type, request_id}` only; details fetched over authenticated in-region API; generic lock-screen text | [FCM 4 KB](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/customize-messages/set-message-type); PDPL commentary |
| Reachability UX | Pre-flight device chip ("Pixel 8 · last seen 2 min") and ack timers: no ack in ~10 s → "Phone not responding — open the app or use callback/browser"; tapped but no dialing in ~30 s → "Waiting on your phone"; app reports `locked`, `notifications_off` and `priority_downgraded` reasons | [FCM delivery](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/understand-delivery); [KeyguardManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/KeyguardManager) |
| Multi-device | Target the preferred telephony-capable device; show a chooser when ambiguous; never ring-all for outbound SIM calls; first accept cancels the rest | Chrome device chooser; Dialpad preferred device |
| Android dial path | Heads-up notification with a "Call" action → `placeCall` with `CALL_PHONE` (1 tap) or `ACTION_DIAL` fallback (2 taps); `shortService` FGS + `TelephonyCallback`; FSI only if the user grants it | [TelecomManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/telecom/TelecomManager); [Android 14](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/behavior-changes-14) |
| iOS dial path | Time Sensitive alert → foreground → `tel:` confirm → `CXCallObserver`; report on return to foreground; accept coarser data | [Apple Phone Links](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/featuredarticles/iPhoneURLScheme_Reference/PhoneLinks/PhoneLinks.html); [CXCallObserver](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/callkit/cxcallobserver) |
| Play compliance | Keep `READ_CALL_LOG` out of the main ecosystem app, in an optional tracker module or managed-Play private app under the CRM exception; no in-app recording on Play builds | [Play SMS/Call Log](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10208820) |
| Fallbacks | Offer "Call via: My phone · This browser · Callback to my number" with a remembered default; the bridge is the reliable fallback when the phone is unreachable | [RingCentral RingOut](https://support.ringcentral.com/app/phone/desktop-web/enabling-using-ringout-ringcentral-app-desktop-web.html); [HubSpot](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/calling/use-the-calling-tool) |
| Web widget | Shell-level, cross-product floating card keyed by `request_id`, rehydrated on reload, docked/expanded/pill states, non-drag move controls, `aria-live` status, BroadcastChannel tab fan-out with a leader tab | [WCAG 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html); [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Broadcast_Channel_API) |
| Logging | Always open a post-call log form on the web (Pipedrive pattern), pre-filled with whatever the phone measured; label Android durations as "includes ringing" unless call-log data is present; log only CRM-matched numbers | [Pipedrive](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/web-to-mobile-calling); [Kylas](https://support.kylas.io/portal/en/kb/articles/how-to-sync-the-mobile-phone-call-logs-with-kylas) |
| Governance | Layered enablement (entitlement → tenant → role → device opt-in); per-tenant quotas and rate limits on call commands; immutable audit of who sent which call to which device for which record | Salesforce quotas; LeadSquared role policies |
| Onboarding | Permission and battery health check in the app, reported to an admin dashboard (installed, notifications, CALL_PHONE, battery exemption, OEM autostart) | [Salestrail](https://www.salestrail.io/knowledge-base/calls-not-logged-in-the-salestrail-app-causes-and-solutions); [dontkillmyapp](https://dontkillmyapp.com/) |

Several things remain unknown: which device HubSpot and Pipedrive target when several are logged in; whether Android 15/16 permits `placeCall` from a background service with no tap; whether `CXCallObserver` fires while an app is suspended during a cellular call; and exactly which Play permissions carry the CRM asterisk. Each should be settled by device testing or a direct policy check before committing to a design.

## Conclusion

The competitive bar is low. The two global CRMs that offer push-to-own-phone calling document only the happy path, and the Android-first vendors leave reliability to the user's battery settings. The hard engineering is not placing the call. It is **knowing, and telling the web user, what happened**. Push providers are fire-and-forget, the OSes hide call details from non-dialer apps, and app stores penalise the obvious workarounds of silent pushes, full-screen intents and call-log access. A product that treats the request as a tracked command, with acks, short expiry, explicit reason codes and an always-available bridge fallback, can deliver the "ringing on your phone… in call 02:13" experience nobody currently documents, within the rules as they stand.

For a multi-product no-code ecosystem, building these pieces once at platform level pays off well beyond calling. The registry, presence model, command lifecycle and shell-level widget give every product a reliable way to reach the user's phone and show the result back in the browser, under one set of tenant policies and one compliance review. The CRM's call button is the first feature to need it, so it is the right place to start.
